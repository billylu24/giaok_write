         Abstract. Current methods for handwritten Chinese text generation
         primarily focus on generating individual characters. However, for prac-
         tical applications, users often require a handwritten text-line generation
         model to streamline their workﬂows. Generating stylized handwritten
         text-line necessitates capturing both character-level styles (e.g., stroke
         thickness, ink color, and cursive join) and text-line-level styles (e.g., inter-
         character spacing and text-line inclination). To address this challenge,
         we propose a novel cascaded diﬀusion architecture (i.e., Layout-Diﬀ and
         Imitating-Diﬀ) to disentangle these two levels of styles via customized
         template guidance. Speciﬁcally, Layout-Diﬀ assists in synthesizing a tem-
         plate image, where the visual archetypes, rendered in a printed font, are
         well-arranged on a blank canvas. Subsequently, Imitating-Diﬀ transforms
         this synthesized template into a stylized handwritten text-line image. We
         propose a latent aggregation module to eﬃciently incorporate textual
         guidance into Imitating-Diﬀ while reducing computational cost. Further-
         more, we design a high-frequency weighted loss for ﬁne-tuning Imitating-
         Diﬀ in the pixel space to preserve structure details with clarity. Exten-
         sive experiments demonstrate that our method eﬀectively mimics diverse
         handwriting styles while ensuring structural accuracy in generated text
         lines.

    To address these challenges, a straightforward approach is to generate char-
acters independently using a single-character model and manually stitch them
according to a text-line layout (see Fig. 1). However, this method often produces
rigid and unnatural text-lines, as it fails to capture the stylistic cohesion inher-
ent in natural handwriting. Alternatively, directly constructing one generative
model is typically challenging and impractical for precise editing, as it lacks an
explicit representation of layout information. To better capture the spatial stylis-
tic characteristics of text lines, we extend the concept of visual archetypes [31]
from the character level to the text-line level, a construct we term Template.

printed font according to the target layout, thereby disentangling character-level
styles and text-line-level styles to create stylized handwritten text lines. Com-

2     Related Work

Handwritten data can be stored in the form of online trajectory sequences
or oﬄine images. The advantage of online handwritten data lies in its ability
to maintain dynamic trajectory information of the drawing process, while the
advantage of oﬄine handwritten data lies in its more natural representation of
visual features such as stroke thickness and ink color.
Online Handwriting Generation. Most of online handwriting generation
methods are typically based on autoregressive neural network [12, 49], uti-
lizing a mixture of Gaussian distributions to model the movement of pen.
SketchRNN [15], which based on Variational Autoencoders (VAE) [20], adopts
RNN to conduct the task of unconditional sequence data generation. Subse-
quently, a series of works [1, 22, 40–42, 50] focuses on conditional generation, aim-
ing to produce handwriting that encompasses speciﬁc content or diverse writing
styles. SDT [6] leverages the style-disentangled Transformer to more eﬀectively
extract style features from reference samples. In addition, there are studies that
apply diﬀusion models to online sequential data [7, 29, 34], achieving notable
results.
Oﬄine Handwriting Generation. Oﬄine handwriting generation meth-
ods [2, 4, 11, 21, 43] were primarily based on Generative Adversarial Networks
(GANs) in the early days, leveraging adversarial training to create realistic
handwriting samples. Recently, with the development of diﬀusion models, an
increasing number of studies have begun to utilize these models to generate
oﬄine font data [46]. Diﬀusion models oﬀer a novel framework that reﬁnes ran-
dom noise into structured handwriting, allowing for more nuanced control over
style and content. State-of-the-art methods can now extract style information
from a signiﬁcantly limited number of reference samples [5, 9, 30, 47, 52].
Score-Based Diﬀusion Model. Score-based diﬀusion models systematically
perturb data via a forward diﬀusion process, subsequently reversing this pro-
cess to recover the original data [17, 36, 38]. Due to their capability to balance
ﬁdelity and diversity, along with advancements in accelerated sampling [3, 27, 37]
and conditional generation [8, 35], these models have been extensively adopted
across diverse data modalities. We also adopt diﬀusion models as our generative
framework.

4     Methods
4.1   Overview
Our goal is to synthesize handwritten text-line images given an arbitrary length
string A as content and a single style reference image Istyle from a writer ws .

Here, A = {ai }i=1 is a sequence of length L, where each ai is a Unicode character,
encompassing Chinese characters, English letters, digits, punctuation and other
symbols. The generated handwritten text-line images should faithfully capture
the distinctive style of ws while accurately conveying the content of A. Fur-
thermore, the spatial arrangement of characters must be ﬂuid, maintaining the
natural rhythm of handwritten text-line.
    Existing methods are limited to generating single Chinese characters images
and fail to handle more complex text content. To address this limitation, we
propose a cascaded diﬀusion framework that systematically structures the gen-
eration process(see Fig. 3). Speciﬁcally, a layout style encoder and an image style
encoder are employed to capture text-line-level and character-level styles, respec-
tively. These multi-level style features are then injected into the corresponding
UNet model via cross-attention. In Imitating-Diﬀ, template information is fused
with the noisy latent through concatenation. During training, the template is
directly extracted from the input image, whereas during inference, it is synthe-
sized by Layout-Diﬀ.

4.2   Layout-Diﬀ
Text-Line Template. As stated before, the objective of Layout-Diﬀ is to gen-
erate a text-line template, which serves as the content information for Imitating-
Diﬀ. Text-line template comprises the bounding box of each character and their
corresponding standard font images. Speciﬁcally, the bounding box deﬁnes the
position and size of each character within the text-line image. The bounding box
coordinates are converted into normalized values representing the height, width,
the vertical distance from the top of each character to the top of the text line,
and the horizontal distance between the centers of adjacent chactarers. We use
the SimHei font as standard character images. We resize the height and width of
standard character images and embed them into their corresponding bounding
boxes in a blank canvas to construct the standard text-line-level templates.
Module Architecture. The layout of a text line refers to the bounding box
coordinates for all the characters. As shown in Fig. 3, the Layout-Diﬀ ﬁrstly
takes the text input A and the layout of the style reference image Istyle as input,
and predicts the layout of the target text-line image. The generative process is
modeled using the diﬀusion process, with a 1D U-Net network serving as the
denoising model ˆθ .
    More concretely, considering that diﬀerent characters have diﬀerent shapes,
(especially punctuation marks), we set a learnable encoding for each character
class i: Embi ∈ Rd , where d is the dimension of the encoding vector. To account
for the distinct layout styles exhibited by diﬀerent authors, we condition the
model on the layout of the reference image Istyle . Speciﬁcally, the reference
layout information Layoutref , added with the reference character embeddings
Embref , is processed through a 1D layout style encoder Eref , which mirrors the
architecture of the 1D U-Net encoder. The resulting style layout features Fref
are then fed into the denoiser via cross-attention.
Training Objective. We model the reconstruction loss as the average distance
between the predicted layout and the actual counterpart. Here t , ˆθ represents
the added true noise and predicted noise at timestep t, while xt represents the
noisy data at timestep t:

4.3   Imitating-Diﬀ
The objective of Imitating-Diﬀ is to transfer the text-line template image
obtained to a stylized handwritten text-line image. A primary challenge lies in
the signiﬁcant computational cost, considering the enormous size of the text-line
image. To address this, as shown in Fig. 3, our method integrates a pretrained
frozen VAE, a CNN-Transformer style encoder, a latent aggregation module,
and a UNet-based denoiser to form a conditional latent diﬀusion model.
156    H. Wang et al.

Leveraging the pretrained VAE encoder, we reduce the resolution of all text-line
images and templates by a factor of 8, transforming them from 96×2048 pix-
els to a 12×256 latent vectors. This reduction signiﬁcantly saves computational
resources, enhancing both training and inference eﬃciency. The generated latent
features are ﬁnally mapped into the pixel space by the VAE decoder.
    Another challenge is to ensure the accurate positioning and structure of char-
acters, as deﬁned in the template. For single-character generation models like
One-DM [5], directly replacing the character template with the text-line tem-
plate often results in improper allocation of characters, despite the presence of
a content-style aggregation module. To address this issue, we design the Latent
Aggregation Module to enable the model to better incorporate both content and
style information.
Latent Aggregation Module. In the ﬁeld of handwritten text generation,
latent space-based diﬀusion methods [5, 30, 47, 52] integrate text information into
the diﬀusion model via cross-attention. In contrast, pixel space-based diﬀusion
approaches, such as CG-DDPM [47], take a noisy image combined with a ren-
dered glyph image as input to the U-Net. A key innovation of our work lies in
demonstrating the eﬀectiveness of template concatenation in the latent space,
thereby eliminating the computational overhead associated with training a con-
tent encoder and employing additional cross-attention mechanisms.
    As shown in Fig. 3, we compress the template image Itemplate into the latent
template ztemplate using the same pretrained, frozen VAE encoder as latent dif-
fusion model. Both the latent template ztemplate and the latent noise zt are pro-
cessed through separate convolutional layers, after which they are concatenated
to ẑt and fed into the U-Net model for further computation. The latent template
image, with its blurred spatial information, helps guide the layout during the
entire generation process.

5     Experiments
5.1   Experimental Settings
Evaluation Dataset. To evaluate the performance of our method, we use the
CASIA-HWDB2.0-2.2 dataset [25] for training, which contains 52,230 Chinese
text-lines from 1,019 distinct writers. For testing the style transfer capability of
our method, we employ the ICDAR2013 dataset [48], which consists of 10,449
Chinese text-lines from 60 diﬀerent writers. For layout generation, we set the
158    H. Wang et al.

number of bounding boxes in a text line to 32, reﬂecting the typical writing
habits of most individuals. Speciﬁcally, we pad target layouts with zeros and
repeat the previous part of the text line for style layouts. For text-line image
generation, all authentic text-line images are resized to a height of 96 pixels
while maintaining their aspect ratio, and padded to a width of 2048 pixels. For
template and target text-line images, we pad with pixels of value 255, while for
style reference images, we repeat the previous part.
Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate the quality of layout generation based on
multiple factors, including character height, width, vertical spacing, and the
inter-character center distances. The L1 losses for all layout-related metrics are
computed on a normalized image, where the height is scaled to 1.
    To assess the style quality of text-line generation, we adopt three style met-
rics: the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) [16], the Geometry Score (GS) [19]
and the Style Score. For the style score, we train a CNN-Transformer encoder
on the test dataset for style classiﬁcation task. For all style metrics, we ﬁrst
compute the values for each writer in the test dataset, then calculate the mean
across all writers’ values.
    To evaluate the structural accuracy of generated text-lines, we employ two
content metrics: Correct Rate (CR) and Accuracy Rate (AR) [48]. These met-
rics are computed using a pretrained handwritten Chinese text line recognition
model, which transcribes each text line into a character string.
Implementation Details. In all experiments, we use only one style reference
sample to perform one-shot experiments (one text-line for layout and text-line
generation). We train all conditional diﬀusion models on eight A6000 GPUs.
Speciﬁcally, we train the Layout-Diﬀ for 60,000 steps with a batch size of 1024.
The Imitating-Diﬀ is then trained for 200 epochs with a batch size of 20, followed
by ﬁne-tuning for 30 epochs with a batch size of 6. The ﬁne-tuning loss weight
ratio, λlatent is 0.1, λSobel is 0.7 and λall is 0.3. AdamW optimizer with β1 = 0.9
and β2 = 0.999 is used. The learning rate is set as 1e−4 with linear schedule
for both training and ﬁne-tuning. During inference, DDIM is used to speed up
sampling with 25 steps.
Compared Methods. We compare our Imitating-Diﬀ with one-shot handwrit-
ten character generation models, including FontDiﬀuser [46], GAC-Diﬀ [47], and
One-DM [5], all of which are adapted to generate coherent text-line images.
For FontDiﬀuser and GAC-Diﬀ, which already incorporate multi-scale content
aggregation modules (or their variants), we simply replace the character tem-
plate with a text-line template and substitute the font style reference with a
text-line style reference. In the case of One-DM, we introduce additional posi-
tional embeddings (PE) or employ our proposed latent aggregation module (LA)
to generate text-line images.

6   Conclusion and Future Work
In this paper, we propose a novel framework for stylized oﬄine Chinese hand-
written text-line generation that integrates glyph templates with Layout-Diﬀ
and Imitating-Diﬀ to disentangle line- and character-level styles. Through latent
aggregation and high-frequency ﬁne-tuning, the model preserves character struc-
ture while capturing stylistic variation. Future work will explore its extension to
multilingual scripts.
